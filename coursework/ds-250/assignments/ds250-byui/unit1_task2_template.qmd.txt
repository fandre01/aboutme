<!-- Make sure at least one of your charts includes reference marks!! -->

__How does your name at your birth year compare to its use historically? Your must provide a chart. The years labels on your charts should not include a comma.__ 

_The chart shows how my name, “Andre,” has been used over time. I highlighted my birth year, 2000, with a red dashed line. From the chart, we can see that the name was not very popular around my birth year compared to other years. Overall, the popularity of the name changed over time, going up and down, but my birth year shows it was less common. This helps me understand how my name fits into historical trends._

```{python}


# Filter for your name
my_name = df.query('name == "Andre"')

# Create the chart
(
    ggplot(my_name, aes(x='year', y='Total'))
    + geom_line(color='blue')
    + geom_vline(xintercept=2000, color='red', linetype='dashed')
    + scale_x_continuous(limits=[1950, 2020],
                         labels=custom_format('{:.0f}'))  # removes commas
    + geom_text(x=2005, y=1900, label="Andre", color='red')
)

```

This conclusion comes from looking at the historical trend of the name’s popularity._

```{python}
# Q2
# Filter for "Brittany"
brittany = df.query('name == "Brittany"')
popular_year = brittany.loc[brittany['Total'].idxmax(), 'year']
print(popular_year)

(
    ggplot(brittany, aes(x='year', y='Total'))
    + geom_line(color='blue', size=1.2)
    + geom_vline(xintercept=popular_year, color='red', linetype='dashed')
    + labs(
        title='Popularity of the Name "Brittany" Over Time',
        x='Year',
        y='Total'
    )
```

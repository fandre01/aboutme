```{r}
#Create a chart that looks at the distribution of departure delays by age (or age group).
#Do you notice any patterns?
#Create a new column that groups age into 0-10, 11-20, 21-30, and >30

flight_age_group <- age |>
  mutate(
    age_group = case_when(
      age <= 10 ~ "0-10",
      age <= 20 ~ "11-20",
      age <= 30 ~ "21-30",
      TRUE ~ ">30"
    )
  ) |>
select(carrier, age, dep_delay, age_group, air_time)
head(flight_age_group)
```
```{r, warning=FALSE}
chart =(
  ggplot(flight_age_group, aes(y = dep_delay, x = age_group, fill = age_group))
  + geom_boxplot(outlier.shape = NA)
  +coord_cartesian(ylim = c(-10, 60))
  + geom_hline(yintercept = 0.3, color = "red", linetype = "dashed")
  +labs(
    title = "Distribution of Departure Delays by Plane Age Group",
    x = "Plane Age (Years)",
    y = "Departure Delay (Minutes)"
  )
  +theme_minimal()
)

chart
```
#Chart 1- Analysis
Interestingly, the distribution of departure delays remains highly consistent across all plane age groups. The median delay is near zero for all categories, and the spread of the data (IQR) doesn't significantly widen for older aircraft. This suggests that a plane's age is not a strong predictor of departure delays.

#Chart 2 Analysis
Looking at the distribution of plane ages across different carriers, there is a stark contrast in fleet management. Airlines like American Airlines and Envoy Air consistently operate older fleets with medians well above 20 years. Conversely, carriers like JetBlue, Hawaiian, and Virgin America feature significantly younger fleets, with median plane ages under 10 years.
